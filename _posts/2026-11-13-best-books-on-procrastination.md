Putting things off is not one problem. For one person the task is boring, for another it is scary, and for a third the real issue is never switching off. The five books below each give a different answer to the question "why do I do this?", so the list is about matching the explanation to your own situation.

<div class="summary-box" markdown="1">
## In three sentences

These five books explain procrastination as a problem of motivation, of planning your day, of anxiety and guilt, of managing emotions, and of deeper fears. Start with the one whose explanation sounds like you, because a method built for the wrong cause will not help. No book will do the task for you, so pick one idea and try it this week.

**Who this list is not for:** anyone whose delays come with long-lasting low mood, constant anxiety or attention problems. A book is not a substitute for talking to a doctor or therapist about that.
</div>

## Quick picks

- **If you want the science of why people put things off:** *The Procrastination Equation*
- **If you want a short, practical way to organize your day:** *Eat That Frog!*
- **If your delays come from anxiety, perfectionism or never feeling free to rest:** *The Now Habit*
- **If you want a short guide that treats procrastination as an emotion problem:** *Solving the Procrastination Puzzle*
- **If you want to look at the fears underneath the habit:** *Procrastination*

## At a glance

- ***The Procrastination Equation***: a research-based book built around a formula for motivation, by the author of the 2007 research review.
- ***Eat That Frog!*** (2001): a time-management book built on doing your most important task first thing in the morning.
- ***The Now Habit***: a program for people who delay because of anxiety, with a scheduling method called the Unschedule.
- ***Solving the Procrastination Puzzle*** (2013): a concise 128-page guide that argues procrastination is about emotions, not time.
- ***Procrastination***: a counseling-style book, based on the authors' workshops, about the fears behind putting things off. The edition I looked at is the 25th anniversary edition.

Each section below ends with one small thing to try this week. Those suggestions are mine, not the authors'.

## What the research says first

A well-known 2007 review in *Psychological Bulletin* by Piers Steel went through 691 correlations to see what is actually linked to procrastination. Traits such as neuroticism, rebelliousness and sensation seeking showed only a weak connection, while task aversiveness (how unpleasant the task is) and task delay were among the strong and consistent predictors. A separate line of work by Timothy Pychyl and colleagues points to emotions: people put off a task to avoid a bad feeling it brings up. Pychyl puts it as "an emotion-management problem" and not a time-management one. The two ideas do not contradict each other. One looks at how tasks are weighed, the other at how they feel, which is why the books below take such different routes.

## 1. The Procrastination Equation, Piers Steel

{% include book-cover.html asin="0061703613" title="The Procrastination Equation" %}

Written by a business professor who published the research review above. Its central idea is a formula: motivation equals expectancy times value, divided by impulsiveness times delay. In plain words, you are more likely to start when you believe you can do it, the task matters to you, you are less distracted, and the reward is not far away. Publishers Weekly called it an absorbing first book and said Steel looks at procrastination as a mismatch between human evolution and modern society, not simply laziness.

**Best for:** readers who want to understand the "why" with evidence before changing anything, and who like a structured way to see what is holding them back.

**Downside:** Publishers Weekly said Steel is perhaps too abstruse in describing the results of some findings, so expect some heavier passages.

**Try this week:** pick one task you keep avoiding and set a deadline sooner than the real one, then tell someone about it. This follows the "delay" part of the formula.

## 2. Eat That Frog!, Brian Tracy

{% include book-cover.html asin="1444765426" title="Eat That Frog!" %}

First published in 2001. The title comes from a line often credited to Mark Twain: eat a live frog first thing in the morning and nothing worse will happen to you for the rest of the day. For Tracy, the "frog" is your most important task. The book says to plan each day in advance, work from a list and do that task first, before anything else.

**Best for:** people who want simple, ready-to-use rules for organizing a working day and do not need an explanation of why they delay.

**Downside:** one critical reviewer said the book is mostly basic tools for arranging your day, with very little on why people procrastinate, and that it has no citations and some unsourced claims.

**Try this week:** tonight, write down the one task that matters most tomorrow, and make it the first thing you work on.

## 3. The Now Habit, Neil Fiore

{% include book-cover.html asin="1529146682" title="The Now Habit" %}

Fiore, a psychologist, argues that procrastination is often a way to cope with the anxiety attached to a task, not laziness. One summary describes his view that people tie their worth to a task, use perfectionism to raise the stakes of a mistake and then freeze. His method includes the Unschedule: you plan your free time and rest first, and treat guilt-free play as something you have to make time for.

**Best for:** people who delay because they feel anxious about a task, set impossible standards, or feel guilty whenever they are not working.

**Downside:** one reader on a productivity forum did not find it useful because the Unschedule asks you to put routine tasks on a calendar, which did not fit days that are hard to predict.

**Try this week:** put one real break in your calendar before you schedule the work, and keep it.

## 4. Solving the Procrastination Puzzle, Timothy Pychyl

{% include book-cover.html asin="0399168125" title="Solving the Procrastination Puzzle" %}

A concise guide of 128 pages by an associate professor of psychology at Carleton University who has researched procrastination since the mid-1990s. His main claim is that procrastination is an emotion-regulation problem, not a time-management problem: we put things off to avoid feelings such as frustration or resentment. The publisher describes the book as a revision of a self-published hit that offers strategies based on current psychological research.

**Best for:** readers who want a short book and who recognize "I avoid it because it feels bad."

**Downside:** the reviews I found were mostly summaries, so there is little independent criticism to report. At 128 pages it is a concise guide, not a full program, so it will not go as deep as a longer book.

**Try this week:** before you start a task you have been avoiding, name the feeling it brings up (boredom, worry, irritation), then work on it for five minutes anyway.

## 5. Procrastination, Jane Burka and Lenora Yuen

{% include book-cover.html asin="0738211702" title="Procrastination" %}

The full title is *Procrastination: Why You Do It, What to Do About It Now*. Based on the authors' workshops and counseling experience, it looks at the reasons we put things off, including fears of failure, success, control, separation and attachment, and at their roots in childhood and adult life. It also gives tips for living and working with someone who procrastinates.

**Best for:** readers who suspect that fear sits underneath their habit, and who want a more personal, reflective approach than a productivity system.

**Downside:** I found little independent criticism, and the main source I could check is the publisher's own description. Because the method comes from the authors' counseling experience and not from one research program, treat the list of fears as a framework to test on yourself.

**Try this week:** write one sentence about what you are afraid might happen if you did the avoided task badly, and another about what might happen if you did it well.

## Where to start

If you are not sure why you put things off, start with *Solving the Procrastination Puzzle*, since it is the shortest, or *The Procrastination Equation* if you prefer evidence. If the problem is a messy day, start with *Eat That Frog!*. If anxiety or guilt is the main thing, start with *The Now Habit*, and if you suspect fear, try *Procrastination*.
